I don't know how I became this way, but I've never been a chill person. I don't know how to relax. My husband is like this too; we call it "the curse".

When I was 5 I had a recurring nightmare. In that nightmare, I was at the starting line of a 100m sprint, along with at least 20 others. I knew, somehow, that if I didn't win the race I would die. Then some whistle would blow, we'd leap into motion in slo-mo, the race would proceed, and I'd wake up in tears before anyone ever crossed the finish line.

The curse (as I understand it) is [never being quite satisfied](https://youtu.be/YCxxWli-gkA?si=p_8gqPjZ2FnnqnJ4&t=98). Or if you do feel that satisfaction, it only lasts a brief moment and then that's your baseline and now you're hunting for the next thing. Living in New York doesn't help! Online discourse about how [short](https://waitbutwhy.com/2014/05/life-weeks.html) life is doesn't help!
### Breaking the curse... maybe

I know it sounds bad, but I don't know if I actually want to break the curse. It has done so much for me. My goal-oriented never-stopping-for-a-break self is often exhausting to be around, but I'm so used to it at this point (27 years in) that I'm actually quite comfortable being this way. I am super proud of the life I've built being cursed. 

I got my dream job out of college (in part, thanks to the 5 year plan I wrote my freshman year), married my first boyfriend, I've spent significant time (>1 year) in 4 cities across 3 continents, and I generally have been pretty good at building a community wherever I go. It's something I'm grateful for!

Despite this, I will always want more (see: [bisexual greed](https://www.glamourmagazine.co.uk/article/greedy-bisexual-betahny-rutter-extract)). I logically recognize the insane privilege I have already, but there is also the deep deep desire to "realize my truest potential" in this lifetime (and win the non-existent race... another post for another time).

In 2023, my new year's resolution was to "not make any resolutions". That lasted like a week? I love having a juicy goal to work towards. It fuels me, and if I ever stop in place, I'm certain the dual specters of boredom and dissatisfaction will catch up to me. So maybe I'll keep the curse around for a bit longer.
