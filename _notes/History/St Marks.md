## Dutch New Amsterdam (1640–1664)

- Before Peter Stuyvesant came Director General Willem Kieft
- Kieft started brutal wars with native Americans
- Many colonists gave up and went back to Europe, leaving only **250–300 men able to bear arms**

### Peter Stuyvesant
- [Calvinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Christianity) who lost his right leg to a Spanish cannonball in **1644** during a sea battle in the Caribbean
- Director General of New Netherland
- **1651**: bought land for a *bouwerie* (old Dutch word for farm, the root for "Bowery") from Dutch West India Company
- Built a country refuge about two miles north of town, to raise his family away from New Amsterdam's "pirates and prostitutes"
- [New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam#/media/File:New_York_City_-_Manhattan_-_Lower_Manhattan_(cropped).PNG) was only the Southern tip of Manhattan
- **1660**: built a Dutch Reformed family chapel at what's now **10th Street & 2nd Avenue**, so the family could skip the long Sunday trip to church at the island's tip. This is the site of today's **St. Mark's Church in-the-Bowery**.
- **1664**: the poorly defended Dutch colony was seized by the English. New Amsterdam was renamed **New York** after the Duke of York (later James II & VII).
- Died in **1672** and is buried in a vault under the chapel. Hated the English and banned English from being spoken in his house.

## Early Republic (1804–1854)
- St. Marks Pl was renamed in 1835 by a developer named Thomas E. Davis, who wanted to make the neighborhood feel more upper class. 

### Eliza Schuyler Hamilton
- Alexander Hamilton died in **1804**, leaving Eliza to pay his debts. Their old house in Upper Manhattan was sold at public auction (**The Grange** was a 35-acre estate).
  - The executors decided she couldn't be publicly dispossessed, so they bought it themselves and sold it back to her at **half price**.
- **November 1833** (age 76): she resold The Grange for **$25,000** (~$834K in 2025).
  - Used the money to buy a townhouse, the **Hamilton-Holly House** (4 St. Marks Pl), where she lived for nine years with two grown children (Alexander Hamilton Jr. and Eliza Hamilton Holly) and their spouses.
- **1848**: left NYC for Washington, D.C., living with her widowed daughter Eliza until **1854**.

## Little Germany / Kleindeutschland (1840–1904)

### Growth
- **By 1845**: already the largest German-American neighborhood in New York
- **By 1855**: its German population had more than quadrupled, pushing out the American-born workers who first moved into the area's new housing
- **By 1855**: NYC had the **third-largest German population of any city in the world**, behind only Berlin and Vienna
- **Early 1900s**: almost **50,000** residents

### Lifestyle
- German immigrants took over rooming houses and converted single-family buildings (e.g., the Urchs family) 
- Middle-class trades: dressmaking, baking, brewing, piano tuning (cool jobs)
- Social life centered on "beer halls" and parties
- **No. 12**: headquarters of the German Shooting Society. The façade still reads *"Einigkeit Macht Stark"* ("Unity Makes Strength").
- **No. 19–21, 1874**: the Arion Club (a German music society) threw a party where members wore **dolphin-shaped hats**.
- **No. 19–21, 1887**: George Ehret (the brewer; spelled "Ehert" in the source) bought the property and combined **nos. 19, 21, and 23** into **Arlington Hall**, a ballroom and community hall.

### The *General Slocum* disaster (June 15, 1904)
- **St. Mark's Evangelical Lutheran Church** chartered the steamship for **$350** for its annual outing
- **~1,400 passengers**, mostly women and children
- Planned route: up the East River and east across Long Island Sound to a picnic on Long Island
- Ship caught fire and sank, 1000+ people lost their lives, deadliest single-day catastrophe in NYC until 9/11 
- The neighborhood had already been declining as residents moved uptown, and after the disaster most remaining Germans moved uptown
