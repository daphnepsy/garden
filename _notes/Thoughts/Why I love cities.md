I love cities. With the exception of college (I went to school in Claremont, California), I've always lived in cities: Hong Kong, San Francisco, London, New York.

Each city is different. Cities have a personality, and the people that you run into everyday just walking around shape you in such unexpected ways. They impart their energy onto you, imprint little bits of their soul, and you all exist as molecules in a container, bouncing off each other like Brownian motion.

If you're like me (easily influenced), your city will accentuate certain traits while attenuating others. It's like the Pokémon [Eeveelutions](https://pokemon.fandom.com/wiki/Eeveelution). HongKong!Daphne is not SanFrancisco!Daphne is not London!Daphne. We even sound different (I tend to "code-switch" my accent to match those I'm around).

Some thoughts on each city, and how they made me:
- **Hong Kong**: I grew up here, ages 0-18. It taught me the Confucian values I still (try) to uphold: to respect my elders, education and hard work, stability and order, etc. It taught me the importance of family and community, and only as an adult, did I appreciate how kind and caring of a world I grew up in. It was an incubator, where I was taught to trust strangers, to think of humans as fundamentally kind, and honestly, to be a bit naive.
- **San Francisco**: Came here after college, ages 22-25. I arrived after a 4 month "social drought" in Chicago, where I knew practically no one. I was determined to make the most of SF, to meet as many people as I could. It was a crazy city, and filled me with so much energy. I loved the nature (surfing, skiing, camping, hiking, running through GGP and along the Embarcadero). I loved the whimsy (Bay to Breakers) and the weird (Fulton Street Fair, Burning Man). I remember going out in Berlin (Berghain! KitKatClub!) and realizing I had seen it all already back home, and I couldn't possibly go to a crazier party. Over the years, I started feeling stranger. My friends and I would walk past the drug-addled and the homeless people who filled the neighborhoods around Union Square, SOMA, Civic Center, and pretend we didn't see them. I disliked the social default to disregard their humanity.
- **London**: ages 25-26. Frankly, I had a harder time here. In part because the culture and the lifestyle was so different than the one I experienced in the US, and the personalities I encountered in this city were so different too. I'll talk about this another time.
