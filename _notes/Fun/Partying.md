I love to party, but more than that I love throwing a good party. I love bringing people together and having my friends become friends (and lovers, it has happened before) and bringing more joy and delight into this world. Hosting a successful party is euphoric. I love cleaning up cups and wiping down tables in the morning. I love the next-day recap at lunch (breakfast is too early).

Never do I feel more connected to people than when I have had 2 drinks (I'm a lightweight). I didn't drink / severely limited my consumption to 1-2 drinks a week for ~6 months before my wedding and it sucked.
## Why themed parties are the best

Themed parties are ideal. Priya Parker (of *The Art of Gathering*) discusses that parties need a [purpose](https://www.priyaparker.com/art-of-gathering-newsletter/the-lighter-side-of-gathering) . And when you ask your guests to do something for your party (bring a pie! wear a Shrek-themed costume! read this essay!), you may be inconveniencing them, but you're also creating a shared context for all those present, one that is joyful and rare. High effort high reward.

That goes for the host as well. The more effort/energy you put in, the more your guests will reflect the effort/energy back at you. Not every gathering has to be high effort (!= high cost, btw), but I strongly believe that a high effort gathering is more memorable and delightful for everyone involved. And we all need more themed parties in our lives.

**Exhibit A**: my going away "funeral", in 2024, before I left San Francisco (my F-1 visa ran out). We built a cardboard coffin and painted it black. Friends placed white roses on my body, there were poetry readings and eulogies, I drank and cried. It was incredibly participatory and more importantly fun! A guest told me later it was "a paradigm shift on what \[they\] believed parties could be". Still not over it.
